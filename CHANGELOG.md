### Fixed

- **Answer checker, first correction (ISSUES #19).** `phase3c/rescore.py` removed a sentence's final period and then stripped the closing `$` of a math span, so a correct answer written as a sentence was lost ("... is $-2 + 7i$." was scored as "7"). Fixed: the `$math$` span is located before any `$` is stripped, and a stray `</think>` token inside the answer is removed. 21 unit tests (`phase3c/test_rescore.py`) at that point, 9 of which fail on the old code; the checker now has 46.
- **Answer checker, second correction (ISSUES #19).** The first correction still misread a whole family of correct answers. When the model wrote `The value of $x$ is **83**`, the checker took the `$x$` and scored "x"; a value followed by a unit was glued to it (`$\frac{16}{27}$ gallons` became `\frac{16}{27}gallons`); and a money answer written `$\$10$` could not match a gold answer of "10". A test author found the first case. The final checker applies the same rules to every arm and never looks at the gold answer: a `$math$` span that ends the answer is the answer; a span followed by nothing but a linking word ("is", "is equal to", "equals"; up to four words may stand before it) and a plain number or fraction states that value; any other span is the answer (`$\frac{13}{15}$ of the pie` stays the fraction), and an ordinal span (`the $12^{th}$ grade`) is its number; an escaped dollar sign is a dollar sign. Measured answer by answer on the 20,218 saved answers of phases 3c (the fifteen valid 500-problem runs), 8, 11 and 5 — right answers under four rules: 4,681 under the rule as first committed (the first-reported scores), 5,226 under the first re-score of 2026-11-23/24, 5,275 under the first correction as last refined, 5,795 under the final checker. Final against the committed rule: 1,125 answers turn from wrong to right and 11 from right to wrong. Of the 11, four were accidental hits of the old rule, which took a digit out of text that was not an answer (`Wait, $ax + 2a$` read as 2); the other seven are misreads that remain in the final checker: a sentence inside display math (`$$ 42! \text{ has exactly 9 trailing zeroes.} $$` is read as one string); a value offered in two equal forms (the second is taken, and the matcher cannot see that the two are equal: two answers); `There are $76^\circ$ degrees in $\angle B$.` (read as "angle B"); `There are 7 such positive integers $n$.` (read as n); `There are 4 possible values for $x$.` (read as x); and one looping, cut-off text in which the right value stands in an earlier line. The checker is frozen here; the seven are on record so that no quoted score is read as exact to the last answer (each is one answer of a 500-problem run, 0.2 points). Final against the first re-score: 570 from wrong to right and 1 from right to wrong — the one a false positive the old rule had let through (a model wrote "4.76" after `$\frac{9}{2}$ expressed as a decimal is`; the old checker took the fraction and scored it right against a gold of 4.5). math-verify, given only the last line of each of the 570, agrees with 566; of the other four, one is a correct point (a tuple it could not parse) and three are cut-off, looping texts that happen to contain the right value in an earlier line. Final against the first correction as last refined: 520 right, none wrong. Every saved run was re-scored again (the current values are under Added), and every re-scored file records the checker version (`rescored_with`); the checker's unit tests grew from 21 to 46 (`phase3c/test_rescore.py`). Three comparisons change label. The unedited model with thinking switched off against the published edit goes from "not distinguishable" to 7.2 points behind (−7.2, interval −11.8 to −2.8; not distinguishable under math-verify: −1.0, interval −6.2 to 4.2). The phase-10 edit against its 6-pass GLM-Flash run goes from "not distinguishable" to +4.4 (interval 0.2 to 8.6; p = 0.049 — at the edge of what 500 problems can tell apart). The MiMo-flash edit against the GLM-Flash edit goes from "not distinguishable" to +4.6 (interval 0.6 to 8.6; p = 0.036).
