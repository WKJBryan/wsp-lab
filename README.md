# wsp-lab

A complete replication, stress-test, and extension of **weight-space programming** — the claim, from the MIT CSAIL post [*Sparks of Weight-Space Programming*](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming) (Nov 2026), that an agentic LLM given a neural network's raw weights can diagnose and repair it through direct weight edits alone: no gradient updates, no fine-tuning, just arithmetic on parameter tensors.

**All model evaluation ran locally on one RTX 5090. The editor models ran through the host's configured providers; some sessions used paid API paths (ISSUES #25). Every final is reproducible from this repo; the practice passes of phases 4 to 10 are not, because their generations were not saved.**

The original used one editor (OpenAI gpt-6-astra, hinted, 50 evaluation passes) and released [full logs](https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs) — but no cross-editor replication existed, no random-edit control had been run, and (as we verified from their logs) the headline repair was **hinted** in the initial task instructions, with the no-hint condition never tested at full budget. This repo adds the cross-editor replications (phase 4) and the random-edit control, and in phase 10 offered a blind editor all 50 passes. It leaves the hint question open: phase 14 ran the experiment that would isolate the hint — a blind editor under the original authors' rules, with in-distribution practice — and that editor did not reach the original's edit (11.0% against 54.2%), so what the hint buys is still not separated from the editor's size and the smaller budget (finding 3).

## TL;DR — the final scoreboard

Target: Qwen3.5-0.8B, whose "thinking" degenerates into infinite `Wait, no.` loops (stock scores **9.4%** on MATH-500; 91% of generations hit the token cap). All arms share the same 500 problems, engine (vLLM) and scorer (`phase3c/rescore.py` with the matcher `phase3b/scorer.py`), with blind editors unless noted — except the starred phase-3a row, which carries its own caveat.

Scores re-scored 2026-11-26 with the final answer checker (ISSUES #19, #30, #31; the rules of #19 date from 2026-11-24). Calibrated 2026-11-24/25 on the original authors' protocol and grader, and re-graded 2026-11-26: our stack reads 8.8% for the unedited model against 7.2% outside (openwsp), 51.8% for the published edit against 50.6% (openwsp) and 48.1% (the original authors), and 47.0% for the unedited model with thinking switched off against 46.8% — the harness reproduces the outside numbers. Scores in this repository are read by our own matcher, which is now within a point of math-verify: on the calibration runs' boxed answers it reads the same as math-verify for the unedited model (8.8%), 0.2 points lower for the published edit and 1.0 point lower for the unedited model with thinking switched off (4.4 and 5.0 points lower before the third rule), and never higher; read by the repository's usual rule, the same published-edit run gives 54.4% (ISSUES #30). So on the same answers the two graders now agree to within a point, while the two protocols read the same edit a few points apart: on our question the published edit scores 54.2% (48.6% under the checker of 2026-11-24), above the outside readings of 48.1, 50.6 and 51.0, and on the original authors' question 51.8% under their grader (51.0% before) or 51.6% under our matcher on the same boxed answers (46.6% before). Every comparison inside the repository uses the same matcher.

## What we learned — results and thoughts

1. **The core claim is real and robust.** Ten of fourteen blind editor sessions shipped a repair edit, and all ten used the same weight row (the stop-thinking token's embedding row), scaled about ×1.5. They came from four blind editor models — GLM-5.3, GLM-5.3-Flash, mimo-v2.6-flash and mimo-v2.6-pro — working with no hints and no training. (Phase 14, 2026-11-26, adds two sessions under the original authors' rules: its first editor shipped no edit, and its second shipped one on a different tensor, scoring 11.0%.) When independent surgeons converge on one knob out of 873 million, that's the signature of a real, fundamental target. **Restated by phase 8:** what they converged on is at most a decoding setting stored in the weights — under neither grader does the weight edit score above a constant added to the stop-thinking token's score while generating, with the weights untouched (44.8% against the edit's 40.6% under our checker, within about 4 points and at the edge of what 500 problems can tell apart: EXCEEDS; MATCHES under the second grader; ISSUES #34) — and none of the blind edits beats the unedited model with thinking switched off (46.8% against a best blind edit of 42.2%). The original authors' published edit does beat it: 54.2% against 46.8%, a gap of 7.4 points under our checker (95% interval 2.8 to 12.0), though not distinguishable under the second grader (math-verify).
2. **But it's repair, not enhancement.** The edits unstick a broken "stop thinking" switch; they don't add intelligence. A healthy stock model with thinking switched off scores 46.8%; the best blind repair reaches 42.2%, 4.6 points lower (95% interval 0.2 to 9.0, at the edge of what 500 problems can tell apart). Nobody — including the original — has shown an ability *gain* from weight surgery here. (One number now points the other way and is left open: under our checker the original's published edit scores 7.4 points above that thinking-off baseline — finding 1 — though the second grader cannot tell the two apart.)
3. **The gap to the original is not a measurement of the hint.** The blind editors that converged on the stop-token row score 37.2% to 42.2%, and the original authors' hinted editor 54.2% on our stack (48.1% on theirs), but the two experiments differ in at least four ways at once, so the gap cannot be attributed to any one of them (ISSUES #23). Their task instructions named the failure (the model repeats itself in its thinking). Their task instructions and harness forbade the quick fix — edits to the embedding or output layer, skipping or closing thinking early, and multiplying a whole tensor by a constant — and the edit every blind editor here converged on is a rescaling of rows in that embedding. Their editor practised on 7,469 GSM8K, 7,275 MATH and 714 AIME problems, up to 512 per pass at the full 32,768-token cap, over 50 passes; ours practised on 64 GSM8K problems per pass at an 8,192-token cap. And their editor was a frontier model (gpt-6-astra); ours were small local-tier models. What is measured is a quick fix against a deeper edit under different rules, practice data and editor models. The experiment that would isolate the hint — a blind editor under the original rules, with in-distribution practice — has now been run once (phase 14), and the blind editor did not reach the original's edit: held to the original authors' rules and practising on 128 MATH training problems per pass at the full 32,768-token cap, GLM-5.3-Flash used 2 of 15 passes, found a different edit (one tensor in layer 0, not the forbidden quick fix) and scored 11.0% — not distinguishable from stock (9.4%; +1.6 points, 95% interval −1.4 to 4.6) and 43.2 points below the published edit (FALLS SHORT). Its edit lets far more generations close their thinking without reliably making them more often right. So a small blind editor, held to the original rules and practising in distribution, does not reach it; still unseparated are the hint, the editor's size and the smaller budget — the editor that ran was the protocol's fallback (its first choice could not run its session), and it stopped by its own choice. More budget alone did not close the gap: offered 50 passes, a blind editor used 6 and scored 41.6% — 4.4 points above its 6-pass GLM-Flash run (95% interval 0.2 to 8.6, at the edge of what 500 problems can tell apart), indistinguishable from the MiMo-pro edit (+1.0, interval −3.2 to 5.0), and 4.4 of the 17.0 points closer to the published edit — 26% of the way, where a third would be 5.7 (phase 10).
4. **Committees did not beat single editors — in five single sessions.** None of the five phase-9 committees that shipped an edit (of eight conditions run) beat the single editor it is compared with. Three were far worse: the GLM-5.3-Flash team 17.0% against 37.2% (−20.2), the interleaved pair 13.0% against 37.2% (−24.2), the mimo-flash team 9.2% against 42.2% (−33.0). The GLM-5.3 team was not distinguishable from its single editor (35.2% against 31.8%), and the mixed team scored 33.2%, 9.0 points below the best single editor (42.2%; 95% interval −13.0 to −5.0). Each committee is one session, one seed, so this is a pattern noticed after the results. The committees did produce the phase's most sophisticated *methods* (untied heads, escalation gates, a depth-gated additive mechanism) and its most cautious *doses*; that they under-dose, and that gentle doses die on hard problems, is a reading offered after the results. Red-teams were right about every risk they measured — and wrong about which risk mattered.
5. **Session format beats model tier — in these sessions.** mimo tiers could not hold a whole multi-role session (empty reports, leaked reasoning) yet produced the best red-team work of the project when given one focused seat. The harness around a model matters as much as the model.
6. **The loudest scientific killer was measurement, not models.** Our biggest wrong conclusions came from our own rig: a decoding regime in which nothing could succeed, justified by a model-card sentence that is not on the card (ISSUES #8, #26), a scorer that silently failed on Windows, an `ast.parse` check that doesn't catch repeated keywords. (The last was caught by the *editor models*, twice.) The latest, found 2026-11-24, was our own answer checker, which had been misreading correct answers written in a sentence (two more weaknesses of it were fixed on 2026-11-26, ISSUES #30, #31) — the published edit reads 54.2% under the final answer checker against the 39.4% first reported, and the best blind edit 42.2% against 27.8% (ISSUES #19). When results look wrong, suspect the ruler first — the user's instinct on this was the project's most valuable instrument.
7. **Symptoms are self-hinting; silence is the frontier.** Blind discovery worked because loops shout in the outputs. The original's own weights-only sycophancy hunt failed 4× — because flattery leaves no visible trace. Understanding is only needed where watching isn't enough.

## Corrections of 2026-11-23/24

A fresh-eyes audit on 2026-11-23 found several headline claims wrong or unsupported. What changed (ISSUES #19–#31, #34):

- **Answer checker, third rule (ISSUES #30, #31; 2026-11-26).** Two weaknesses of the checker of 2026-11-24 are fixed. *Which line of an unboxed answer is read (ISSUES #31):* it took the last line carrying a phrase like "answer is" even when the model ended on a later line without one, and it read a `\boxed{}` only when its braces were nested one level deep at most; now a complete box anywhere in the answer wins (any depth; blank and unclosed boxes are skipped), and without a box the answer is right if either the marked line or a concluding last line is right. *The comparison with the right answer (ISSUES #30):* it was stricter than the standard grader, math-verify; it now also accepts answers that differ only in formatting — another fraction command or shorthand, a square root without braces, a degree sign, a unit written after the value, thousands separators, a variable in front, a choice letter in parentheses, a number in another base, a mixed number, a power, tuples and fractions compared part by part, a word in other capital letters — and only ever adds matches. Measured answer by answer on all 23,718 saved answers: 943 answers turn from wrong to right and 21 from right to wrong (20 of the 21 were accidental hits of the old line rule on text cut at the length limit or on headings; 1 is a box inside the thinking against a different conclusion); 6,840 right before, 7,762 right now. On the calibration runs' boxed answers the repository's matcher and math-verify now agree on 44 of 44 answers (stock), 319 of 320 (published edit), 491 of 496 (stock with thinking switched off) and 105 of 105 (the phase-14 reference run), and the matcher never accepts an answer math-verify rejects. Every saved run was re-scored again, and every re-scored file carries the stamp `phase3c/rescore.py+phase3b/scorer.py#d74fdebd340f (ISSUES #19, #30, #31)`. As first reported → under the checker of 2026-11-24 → under the final answer checker: stock 6.0% → 8.4% → 9.4%, published edit 39.4% → 48.6% → 54.2%, best blind edit 27.8% → 36.2% → 42.2%. Measured answer by answer on the 20,218 saved answers of phases 3c, 8, 11 and 5: against the rule as first committed, the checker of 2026-11-24 turns 1,125 answers from wrong to right and 11 from right to wrong; against the first re-score, 570 from wrong to right and 1 from right to wrong; against the first correction as last refined, 520 right and none wrong. Seven of the 11 are misreads that remained in that checker, which was frozen at that point (the list is in ISSUES #19), so no quoted score is exact to the last answer — each is one answer of a 500-problem run, 0.2 points. Covered by 46 unit tests then (139 now, and 104 for the matcher), and every saved run re-scored. As first reported → under the checker of 2026-11-24: stock 6.0% → 8.4%, published edit 39.4% → 48.6%, best blind edit 27.8% → 36.2% (9.4%, 54.2% and 42.2% under the final answer checker, first bullet). The wound switches expert selection off as well as removing experts, and no matched control was completed.
- **Selection switched back on (phase 11, 2026-11-24).** The Gemma wound keeps 8 of each layer's 128 experts and still activates 8 per token, so choosing between experts was switched off along with the removal. In a copy of the wound with that choice restored (each token picks its one best kept expert; weights unchanged), the 64-problem practice pass still scores 0 of 64 right — the same as the wound as built, rerun in the same session — and 59 of 64 generations hit the token cap against 64 of 64. Removing the experts is by itself enough to destroy performance on this set; whether the switched-off choice costs anything on top cannot be seen at this floor. The full model scores 55 of 64 (85.9%) on the same problems (47 of 64, 73.4%, under the checker of 2026-11-24). The phase-11 verdict stays NULL.
- **Report counts and verdict words (ISSUES #23, #25, #26, #27).** "10 of 10 blind sessions" is 10 of 14; "Five different editor models" is four blind editor models plus the original authors' hinted one; "0 of 7 committees" is none of the five that shipped an edit, out of eight conditions; "No paid APIs" was wrong; "the hint is worth ~12 points" is not a measurement; a model-card sentence quoted in ISSUES #8 is not on the card; two phase-9 verdict statements (condition 6's label and condition 7's "confirmed") were outside the registered rule.
- **Phase-14 evaluation tool (ISSUES #28).** The tool that scores a phase-14 editor pass had never been run: as committed it had a syntax error on the line that prints its result, and it looked for the base model's fingerprint in the wrong folder; a pass that failed a validity check was not refunded. Found by a dry run with no GPU against a fake engine, before the editor session. Fixed, and hardened after an independent review of its failure paths (the pass is charged only once the tool holds the GPU lock; an interrupted pass is refunded; each attempt keeps its own texts; the candidate's fingerprint is checked again under the lock and covers every file of its folder); `phase14/test_eval_pass.py` (23 tests then, 27 now) runs the real tool end to end against a fake engine, and `test_parse_all.py` parses every Python file of the repository.
- **Calibration (2026-11-24/25).** Under the original authors' protocol and grader our stack reproduces the outside numbers (7.8 / 51.0 / 46.2 against 7.2 / 50.6 / 46.8). Under that protocol the published edit also beats the unedited model with thinking switched off under both graders (+5.4 and +4.8 points; 95% intervals 1.0 to 9.6 and 0.4 to 9.2). And the calibration exposed one more property of our ruler: the matcher is stricter than math-verify by 4 to 5 points on the same boxed answers, mostly because gold answers are written in LaTeX shorthand it does not expand (ISSUES #30). Under the final answer checker (2026-11-26) the same three runs read 8.8 / 51.8 / 47.0 against 7.2 / 50.6 / 46.8, the published edit beats stock with thinking switched off by +5.6 under our matcher (95% interval 1.2 to 10.0) and +4.8 under math-verify (0.4 to 9.4), and the matcher is stricter than math-verify by only 0.2 and 1.0 points (ISSUES #30, closed).

## Integrity practices

- **Pre-registration everywhere:** every phase's thresholds, budgets, and controls committed *before* the run (the git history shows the ordering; it was rewritten on 2026-11-23 to remove personal identifiers, so every commit ID changed); deviations logged in [ISSUES.md](ISSUES.md) with reasons.
- **Harness-enforced passes:** the counter file enforces harness passes and refuses one without a logged hypothesis, but it does not gate an editor's own evaluation scripts (ISSUES #12, #25).
- **Runs are checked before they count:** the three main runners take a shared GPU lock before the engine starts, switch prefix caching off, refuse to overwrite a result file, and write the generations before the result. Every finished run is then checked for two signatures of a corrupted run (`phase3c/runcheck.py`), and the check is recorded inside the result file; a failed check exits with status 2 and keeps the files as evidence (ISSUES #20). Every re-scored file records which checker version produced it.
- **Controls the original skipped:** matched-norm random edits (4.0% — it's the edit's *content*, not its size), untouched baselines, retrain ceilings.
- **Full audit trail:** every editor session's ledger and probe scripts are committed, and so are the generations of the finals (gzipped, for re-scoring without a GPU), of the phase-11 practice passes, and of the phase-14 practice passes and finals (the first attempt's baseline pass included); the practice-pass generations of phases 4 to 10 were not saved.
- **Failures stay in the record:** wrong turns, void runs, and our own bugs are documented, not buried — see [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md) and [ISSUES.md](ISSUES.md) (#4, #8–#34).

## Reproduce

```bash
uv sync                  # torch cu128 (Blackwell) + gymnasium, locked
uv run python phase1/train.py 20      # small-network repair target

```

Phases 3+ additionally need WSL2 + vLLM + CUDA toolkit (see `phase3c/results.md` for the full recipe: vLLM 0.33, CUDA 12.8 toolkit, ninja; models ~1.6 GB each). Re-score any committed run without a GPU — the re-scorers read the gzipped generations directly, so no gunzip is needed — and run the unit tests of the answer checker and of the run-validity checks, which need no GPU either:

uv run python phase3c/rescore.py p4_glmflash     # <run> [<run> ...]; writes <run>_rescored.json

uv run python phase11/rescore.py                 # the phase-11 runs, re-scored in place
