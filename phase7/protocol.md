No model edits in this phase. The deliverable is a reproducible MAP: which parts of which
weight matrices correspond to which behaviors, centered on (but not limited to) the loop
circuit we have isolated. Every claim carries measured evidence; every plot is generated by
a committed script.

## Deliverables

1. **Loop-circuit atlas** (`phase7/ATLAS.md` + PNG heatmaps):
   - Per-layer × per-column alignment map: every down_proj (and up/gate) column in every
     layer scored by its projection onto the loop direction v — where does the loop circuit
     live, layer by layer?
   - Activation confirmation: forward passes over saved STUCK vs CLEAN generations; which
     intermediate neurons actually fire differently (the "neurons light up" picture); overlay
     with the weight-side map (do the aligned columns coincide with the differentially-active
     ones?).
   - Token map: which vocabulary tokens' embedding rows project onto v; which tokens the
     model emits when v-activation is high (the "Wait, …" family?); which output logits move
     when v is perturbed.
2. **Matrix structure survey**: dominant singular subspaces of the big matrices
   (embed_tokens, per-layer projections), with the loop direction's position within them —
   "what combinations of the matrix" carry the load.
3. **Causal spot-checks**: for the 5 strongest claims in the atlas, tiny single-neuron /
   single-column perturbation experiments (weight edits!) with practice-pass verification —
   the map must predict, not just describe. (These draw from Phase 6's budget if any remains;
   otherwise a small dedicated allowance of 6 passes.)

## Method rules

- Weight-space geometry: unlimited CPU analysis.
- Activation probing: forward passes only (no generation) over generation texts already
  saved in phases 3c/5 — stuck set and clean set are fixed lists committed before probing.
- Plots: matplotlib, committed PNGs, scripts committed; no hand-drawn claims.
- Language: every mapped structure gets coordinates (layer, column ids), an effect size,
  and a confidence label (measured / inferred / speculative).

## Addendum — deliverable 3, causal spot-checks on the loop band (written BEFORE any pass, 2026-11-13)

Question: does the map PREDICT? Build a loop brake from our activation map alone (no use of
the original edit's columns or direction) in the upstream band (atlas Finding 8) and see
whether the stock model stops looping. Builder: `build_brake.py` (64 neurons per layer at
L10 and L14, direction d from part 2, in-loop push = 1.5 × the loop–pre residual gap × dose).

Budget: the dedicated allowance of **6 practice passes**, and no other generation of any kind
(no offline tuning — ISSUES #12). Harness: `phase5/eval_pass5.py`, unchanged — 64 GSM8K
practice problems (disjoint from the MATH-500 generations the map was fit on), cap 8192,
pass_index 1 for every arm. Primary metric: `closed_rate` (thinking closes). n = 64, so one
arm has SE ≈ 6 points and a difference SE ≈ 8.

| pass | arm | prediction (fixed now) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | stock | closed 10–30% (phase 4 measured 18.8%) |
| 2 | brake, dose 1.0 | **H1:** closed ≥ stock + 20 → CONFIRMED; < stock + 10 → REFUTED; else INCONCLUSIVE |
| 3 | same neurons + amplitudes, random direction ⟂ d | **H2:** within ±10 of stock → direction-specific; ≥ stock + 20 → H1 is not about d |
| 4 | brake, dose 0.5 | **H3:** closed between stock and the dose-1.0 arm (±6 tolerance) |
| 5 | brake, dose 2.0 | **H3:** ≥ dose 1.0 − 6, OR a cliff (accuracy below stock / degenerate tails) — both recorded, neither refutes |
| 6 | selective brake (zero standing push on clean thinking), dose 1.0 | **H4 (exploratory):** closed within 10 of pass 2 and accuracy ≥ pass 2 |

Order fixed as listed; no arm is added, dropped or re-run for a better number. A pass that

Accuracy, cap-hit, mean tokens and stuck-sample tails are recorded for every arm.
These are practice-scale checks: a CONFIRMED here licenses a MATH-500 final, not a claim.

Pass 2 `orig_prune_quiet`: published edit + the smallest-X% columns zeroed, X = the largest of

Conditional pass 3: if pass 1 falls to <= 28.8% closed, the matched control (two ordinary units).

| # | prediction (fixed now) |
|---|---|
| **S1** | The spike units are not free: zeroing them changes the next token at >= 10x as many positions as zeroing the two ordinary units |
| **S2** | ...but not vital: `orig_prune_spike` still works (closed >= 38.8%) |

## Addendum 9 — knockout test of the subject labels (written BEFORE any run, 2026-11-14)

Question: if the units tagged for a subject are removed, does that subject get worse, and more
than the other subjects do? Forward passes only, stock model, `knockout_subjects.py`.

To keep the test from grading its own homework, the 500 problems are split by index:
even-index problems choose the units, odd-index problems are the test.

- **Units to knock out, per subject (7 subjects):** on the even half, d of every unit for
  "this subject vs the other six" while reading, prompt length regressed out (as in the labels).
  Score = d x sign of the unit's average activation, so a high score means the unit is MORE
  active in size on that subject. The 1,000 highest-scoring units (1.2% of all units) are
  zeroed at every position.
- **Control, per subject:** the same number of units on each floor, drawn at random from the
  units not in that knockout set.
- **Readout:** the stock model's own saved answers to the odd-half problems, first 300
  generated tokens each, teacher-forced. Per subject of the question: extra next-token loss
  caused by the knockout (pruned minus unpruned). "Own" = extra loss on the knocked-out
  subject's text; "others" = mean extra loss over the other six subjects' text.
  Selectivity = own / others.

Known in advance and stated as a limit: a unit that is more active on geometry text removes
more when zeroed on geometry text, so some selectivity is built in. That is still what "this
unit is used for geometry" means, but it is not evidence of a dedicated geometry mechanism.
Also: the labels come from reading the question, the readout is the model writing its answer.

| # | prediction (fixed now) |
|---|---|
| **K1** | Geometry: knocking out its units hurts geometry text more than the other subjects (selectivity > 1) and more selectively than its control (control selectivity within 0.8-1.25) |
| **K2** | The same holds for at least 5 of the 7 subjects |
| **K3** | For at least 4 of the 7 subjects, its own knockout is the most damaging of the seven knockouts on its own text |
| **K4** | The effect is modest: median selectivity across subjects between 1.1 and 2 |

Readings: K1-K3 hold -> the subject labels are causal in the weak sense (remove them and that
subject suffers most), and can be promoted from "goes with" to "used for". K1/K2 fail -> the
labels are readouts of what the question is about, not parts the model leans on for it; they
stay "goes with". Either way no generation run is made in this addendum.
